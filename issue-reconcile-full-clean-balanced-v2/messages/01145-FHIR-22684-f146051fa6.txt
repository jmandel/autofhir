FHIR-22684: Add Consent category to general model

Issue request:
FHIR-22684 was a Published/Persuasive with Modification FHIR-core Consent change request raised against STU3. It asked to fix the Consent general model after query/search parameters changed, including adding Kind/scope, recognizing a patient or third-party grantor, clarifying data, policy-domain location/authority, accountability, dates, actions, and actors.

Initial application:
No source commit explicitly citing FHIR-22684 was found. The likely initial application is the untagged 2019 Consent narrative work: commit bbe7cd28e updated most of the requested context-setting bullets in source/consent/consent-notes.xml, and commit f42fe3d57 preserved that wording while reorganizing the Consent notes after WGM-reviewed changes. That application covered the patient/third-party grantor, data, where, when, action, and actor/accountability bullets, but it did not add the requested Kind/scope bullet.

Additional context:
Consent is active in the current build through source/fhir.ini. Later Consent decisions changed how Kind/scope should be represented: FHIR-31903 eliminated Consent.scope and folded scope-like classification into Consent.category, and FHIR-47213 later clarified that Consent.category is used to show the broad scope of Consent and can support more than one type when needed. FHIR-31906 also explains why the accountability line now names grantee/controller rather than the old performer terminology. No Zulip or Confluence evidence was found that changes the Jira/source conclusion.

AutoFHIR reconciliation:
This commit updates the active Consent notes Privacy Consent General Model by adding the missing context-setting Kind bullet, using current terminology: Consent.category identifies the broad scope of the Consent, with privacy, treatment, and research as examples. It intentionally does not restore the retired Consent.scope element or duplicate the longer Scope and Usage narrative.

Recommendation:
Keep this source fix. It completes the FHIR-22684 general-model request while staying aligned with later scope/category decisions and avoids broader editorial rewrites of the Consent narrative.
