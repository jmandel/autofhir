FHIR-11434: Audit request actionability usage note

Issue request:
FHIR-11434 was a Published/Not Persuasive with Modification FHIR-core workflow change request raised against DSTU2. It argued that prescriptions and orders should remain distinct because a prescription authorizes controlled goods or services while request resources order goods or services. The final decision was to add a Request-pattern usage note explaining that request instances are not always fully specified or immediately actionable, and that order intent authorizes something even when fulfillment may require later information or may never be requested.

Initial application:
The issue was explicitly applied in git-svn import commit 83f1205cc1f012816734622c56ee12d93a24212d, corresponding to svn build@10162, which added the requested three-paragraph actionability note to source/workflow.html under the Request Resource Pattern heading. Later source history modernized names and spelling, including MedicationOrder to MedicationRequest and fulfillment typo cleanup, without changing the approved meaning.
