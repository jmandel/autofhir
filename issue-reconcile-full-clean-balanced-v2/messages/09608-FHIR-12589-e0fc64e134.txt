FHIR-12589: Audit primitive integer plus handling

Issue request:
FHIR-12589 was a Published FHIR-core datatypes change request, resolved Persuasive for DSTU2, asking that the integer and positiveInt regexes allow an optional leading plus sign. The recorded resolution was "allow +" after discussion that +100 is semantically equivalent to 100 but should not be rejected unexpectedly.

Initial application:
The issue was applied by git-svn commit d142af1ffbabbd530f4804e65e3556a022ee9d32, corresponding to Jira's svn @11056 comment. That commit changed the then-authoritative `source/datatypes.html` table to show integer as `[0]|[-+]?[1-9][0-9]*` and positiveInt as `+?[1-9][0-9]*`.

Additional context:
Current datatype publishing uses `source/datatypes.html` with `[%regex ...%]` macros fed by the active primitive spreadsheet source. The current primitive regex source allows `+` for integer and integer64, but positiveInt remains `[1-9][0-9]*` and unsignedInt remains `[0]|([1-9][0-9]*)`. That difference is intentional later context: Published issue FHIR-37368 found that the positiveInt page regex was wrong because it differed from the computable/package representation, and commit 3ef54c37a52ed2d27ea04d457899585052cd4b1f changed the page from `+?[1-9][0-9]*` back to `[1-9][0-9]*`. FHIR-16950 separately confirmed the 32-bit range semantics for positiveInt and unsignedInt and does not require changing sign handling.

AutoFHIR reconciliation:
This commit intentionally makes no source change. The integer portion of FHIR-12589 is already satisfied in the active regex source, and restoring a leading plus for positiveInt would conflict with the later Published FHIR-37368 decision to align positiveInt with the computable representation.

Recommendation:
Keep this audit commit as-is. No FHIR core source follow-up is needed unless a future work-group decision explicitly reverses FHIR-37368 and changes the computable positiveInt or unsignedInt lexical policy.
