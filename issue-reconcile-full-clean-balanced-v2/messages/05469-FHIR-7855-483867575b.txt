FHIR-7855: Audit Condition asserter clarification

Issue request:
FHIR-7855 was a Published Patient Care core Condition clarification from the DSTU1 ballot. It rejected the proposed "person who recorded" wording with modification, requiring Condition.asserter to mean "Individual who is making the condition statement" and adding a usage note that a separate data enterer can be represented with Provenance pointing to the Condition.

Initial application:
The asserter definition was applied in historical commit c6b154ca1b (#7615, #7855, #7859), which changed the old Condition spreadsheet definition from responsibility for assertion in the electronic record to "Individual who is making the condition statement." The Provenance usage note appears in condition-notes.xml and was introduced in the same DSTU1-era notes work, with the exact nurse-on-behalf-of-physician scenario from the Jira comments.

Additional context:
Condition is an active resource in source/fhir.ini. The current authoritative source is structuredefinition-Condition.xml rather than the old spreadsheet. Later core decisions changed the representation but not the seed meaning: FHIR-22270 added Device support and updated the asserter text to "Individual or device that is making the condition statement"; FHIR-39602 restored explicit Condition.recorder and Condition.asserter after the R5 participant experiment; FHIR-41403 added Group as a possible recorder/asserter target; and FHIR-48804 changed only recorder wording while explicitly leaving the asserter definition broad.

AutoFHIR reconciliation:
Made no source change. The current Condition.asserter definition already conveys the FHIR-7855 clarification, adjusted only for later approved non-person asserters, and condition-notes.xml already contains the required Provenance guidance. Reverting to the exact DSTU1 wording would remove later approved Device/Group evolution without improving the seed issue.

Recommendation:
Keep this no-change audit commit as-is. Future Condition.asserter or Condition.recorder clarification should be handled through the later Patient Care issue family, not by reapplying FHIR-7855 literally.
