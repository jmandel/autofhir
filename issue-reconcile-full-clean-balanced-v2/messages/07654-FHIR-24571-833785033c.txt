FHIR-24571: Audit HTTP server safety wording

Issue request:
FHIR-24571 was Published/Persuasive with Modification for R5 and asked the Clinical Safety page to replace ambiguous uses of "server" with "endpoint" where appropriate. The accepted resolution instead chose to clarify that the term means "HTTP server" because that wording was more recognizable than "HTTP endpoint".

Initial application:
Commit 9ae342407271a74f8be031342cf385669c2a8a4e applied the issue to source/safety.html by changing the Search Related Safety Checks introduction from "client and server" to "client and HTTP server" and the search-parameter checklist item from "server's CapabilityStatement" to "HTTP server's CapabilityStatement". No additional Jira-tagged source commit for FHIR-24571 was found in the active safety CodeSystem source, but the current safety-entries CodeSystem now contains the same "HTTP server" clarification for the search-param-names concept through later related safety-check maintenance.

Additional context:
The current safety page is in build scope through source/hierarchy.xml, and source/fhir.ini actively includes codesystem/codesystem-safety-entries.xml as the safety-entries CodeSystem that the safety page links to. Later FHIR-34155 reconciliation refined the search-param-names wording and added the matching CodeSystem concept while preserving "HTTP server". Remaining uses of "server" on the safety page are role labels such as server developers, For Servers, or server-side checklist items; changing those to endpoint would not implement the accepted FHIR-24571 resolution and could make the checklist less clear.

AutoFHIR reconciliation:
Made no source change. The current build-scope safety narrative and formal checklist CodeSystem already carry the intended "HTTP server" clarification where the ambiguous client/server search wording mattered.

Recommendation:
Keep this audit commit as-is. No further FHIR-core source work is recommended for FHIR-24571 unless the work group later decides to replace role-oriented server/client terminology more broadly, which would be a separate policy change.
