FHIR-3059: Audit dispense quantity clarification

Issue request:
FHIR-3059 was a Published/Persuasive Pharmacy change request for MedicationPrescription.quantity, applied for DSTU1, asking that the quantity definition make clear it is the amount to dispense at one time rather than the total amount authorized, which would be quantity multiplied by repeats.

Initial application:
No explicit FHIR-3059-tagged source commit was found. Git pickaxe history shows the original MedicationPrescription source introduced the dispense quantity definition without the "one fill" clarification in 6bf66c167ce30cc7fe9398849a898c25880cdb92, then abce09d8c1edffa07e8bd6fbdf60eaa097da3d91 changed MedicationPrescription.dispense.quantity to "The amount that is to be dispensed for one fill." That wording survived the later MedicationPrescription to MedicationOrder and MedicationRequest renames.

Additional context:
MedicationRequest is active in source/fhir.ini and fhir.ini records that MedicationPrescription became MedicationRequest. The current combined branch has MedicationRequest.dispenseRequest.quantity short text "Amount of medication to supply per dispense" and definition "The amount that is to be dispensed for one fill." The adjacent numberOfRepeatsAllowed definition, clarified by related FHIR-8143, says repeats do not include the original dispense and gives the same 30 tablets plus 3 repeats equals 120 tablets example; FHIR-8143 is already represented on the combined branch and was not republished here. Jira, Zulip, and Confluence searches found no later decision contradicting the current wording.

AutoFHIR reconciliation:
This commit intentionally makes no source change. The active current source already preserves the requested semantic clarification at the renamed MedicationRequest.dispenseRequest.quantity element, and editing it again would only restate the same rule.

Recommendation:
Keep this no-change audit commit as-is. No FHIR core follow-up is needed for FHIR-3059 unless the Pharmacy work group later wants a broader terminology or example change beyond the original one-fill clarification.
