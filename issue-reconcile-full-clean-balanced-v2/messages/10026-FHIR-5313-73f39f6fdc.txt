FHIR-5313: Audit extension elevation policy wording

Issue request:
FHIR-5313 was a Published/Persuasive with Modification FHIR-core ExtensionDefinition ballot comment asking HL7 to soften and clarify the promise that approved extensions would never become core elements. The final resolution kept a strong implementer-protection policy: widely adopted extensions are not elevated into core resources unless the implementer community widely endorses the migration, while draft extensions may move either direction and finalized normative extensions will not be moved.

Initial application:
The issue appears to have been applied in 2015 to source/defining-extensions.html by SVN-derived commits 9ed8870a0e (#5313: Updated wording applied) and 2bca95adc1c (#5313: fix build). Later commits corrected spelling of "labeled", "finalized", and "normative" without changing the policy substance.

Additional context:
The current source still contains the approved policy on the Defining Extensions page, and the page is in the active extensibility documentation surface linked from source/extensibility.html with ExtensionDefinition mapped to the FHIR work group in source/fhir.ini. Jira searches found no later related issue that supersedes the policy, and Zulip/Confluence searches found no directly relevant or conflicting community record.

AutoFHIR reconciliation:
This audit commit intentionally makes no source change. Current source/defining-extensions.html already conveys the FHIR-5313 resolution: no forced migration from widely adopted extensions to core elements without widespread implementer endorsement, plus draft-extension movement flexibility and a normative-extension stability rule. The nearby statement that broadly adopted extensions may be elevated into the base structure remains compatible with that exception rather than contradicting it.

Recommendation:
Keep this audit commit as-is. No follow-up is needed for FHIR-5313 unless the work group separately chooses to modernize the surrounding registry or standards-status terminology beyond this ballot comment.
